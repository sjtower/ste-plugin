# Background and licence position

## History

In the late 1970s the European airline industry asked for a standard form of
English for aircraft maintenance documentation. Maintenance engineers around the
world read these manuals, and most of them do not speak English as a first
language. An ambiguous instruction in a maintenance manual is a safety problem.

The European Association of Aerospace Industries (AECMA) produced the first
guide. The standard is now ASD-STE100. The STE Maintenance Group (STEMG)
maintains it. STEMG is part of the AeroSpace and Defence Industries Association
of Europe (ASD), in Brussels.

The current version is **Issue 9**, released on **15 January 2025**.

Use has spread past aerospace into defence, rail, medical devices, and heavy
machinery. More recently it has spread into software documentation and
machine-facing text.

## Structure of the specification

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Part 1 | 53 writing rules in 9 sections |
| Part 2 | Approximately 900 approved words and 1,200 non-approved words with alternatives |

Each approved word has one meaning and one part of speech. Writers may also use
the technical names and technical verbs that their product needs.

## Licence position of this plugin

**ASD owns the copyright of ASD-STE100 in full.** ASD holds EU Trade Mark
017966390.

This plugin:

- **Does not** contain Part 2, in whole or in part.
- **Does not** reproduce the text of the specification.
- **Does** paraphrase the Part 1 rules in the author's own words, with original
  examples, as a study and writing aid.
- **Is not** affiliated with, endorsed by, or approved by ASD or STEMG.

The plugin therefore **cannot verify compliance**. It checks the mechanical
rules that can be measured without the dictionary. A clean report from
`ste-check` means the text passed those mechanical checks. It does not mean the
text is STE-compliant.

## How to get the specification

ASD gives the specification at no cost after registration:

<https://asd-ste100.org>

If you need to certify compliance, use a licensed STE checker and a human
reviewer. Several commercial checkers hold a licence for the dictionary.
